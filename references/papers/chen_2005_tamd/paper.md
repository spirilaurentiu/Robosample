# Application of Torsion Angle Molecular Dynamics for Efficient Sampling of Protein Conformations

Jianhan Chen, Wonpil Im, Charles L. Brooks III. J Comput Chem 26: 1565-1578, 2005. DOI 10.1002/jcc.20293.

## Abstract

We investigate the application of torsion angle molecular dynamics (TAMD) to augment conformational sampling of peptides and proteins. Interesting conformational changes in proteins mainly involve torsional degrees of freedom. Carrying out molecular dynamics in torsion space not only explicitly samples the most relevant degrees of freedom, but also allows larger integration time steps with elimination of the bond and angle degrees of freedom. However, the covalent geometry must be fixed during internal-coordinate dynamics, which can introduce severe distortions to the underlying potential surface in modern Cartesian-based protein force fields. A "projection" approach (Katritch et al. 2003) is extended to construct an accurate internal coordinate force field (ICFF) from a source Cartesian force field. Torsion crossterm corrections constructed from local molecular fragments, together with softened van der Waals and electrostatic interactions, are used to recover the potential surface and incorporate implicit bond and angle flexibility. MD simulations of dipeptide models demonstrate that full flexibility in both backbone $\phi/\psi$ and side chain $\chi_1$ angles is virtually restored. The efficacy of TAMD is further examined by folding simulations of small peptides and refinement of protein NMR structures, showing several-fold increase in sampling efficiency. The study also reveals intrinsic properties of internal-coordinate dynamics, beyond energy conservation, that limit the maximum integration time step.

Key words: force field; generalized Born; implicit solvent; internal coordinate; NMR refinement; replica exchange; structure prediction.

## Introduction

Sampling is a major bottleneck in MD studies. Traditional MD in Cartesian coordinates is limited to femtosecond time steps by high-frequency bond and angle degrees of freedom, which are only mildly excited at room temperature and have little impact on slow conformational transitions. A popular approach to increase the time step is to eliminate the hardest degrees of freedom: SHAKE constrains bond lengths iteratively but cannot handle networks of holonomic constraints efficiently, so it is typically applied only to bonds involving hydrogen (allowing 1 fs -> 2 fs). Other approaches include multiple-time-scale methods and Langevin stabilization.

A particularly attractive alternative is to solve the equations of motion directly in torsion space, eliminating bond and angle DOF entirely and allowing much larger time steps. The equations of motion for such an internal-variable system are given by eq:1 (see equations.md). The number of torsional DOF $n$ is often much smaller than the $3N-6$ Cartesian internal DOF. Because the mass matrix is non-diagonal, direct solution scales as $O(n^3)$; this limited applicability until linear-scaling algorithms were developed. Jain et al. described a fast recursive algorithm based on the Newton-Euler inverse mass operator (NEIMO) suitable for TAMD of macromolecules using Cartesian-based force fields.

TAMD has been applied to equilibrium properties of large systems, nucleic acid modeling, and NMR/X-ray refinement (simulated annealing in torsion space is now standard for NMR structure calculation). These typically use simplified force fields (ignoring electrostatics, purely repulsive soft-sphere interactions). Initial application to general-purpose MD with realistic all-atom force fields achieved only limited success, mainly due to consequences of the covalent rigid-body approximation.

**The rigid-geometry problem.** Fixing covalent geometry during internal-coordinate dynamics causes: (1) 1-4 van der Waals repulsions (atoms separated by three bonds) are not relaxed, raising rotational barriers; (2) severe clashes between atoms separated by more than three bonds create prohibitively high barriers; (3) identifying the optimal fixed covalent geometry is problematic and application-dependent. For the alanine dipeptide, fixing the geometry both distorts energy minima and dramatically elevates barriers (e.g., barrier near $(\phi,\psi)=(0^\circ,0^\circ)$ from an N-terminal carbonyl O / C-terminal amide H clash 6 bonds apart; barrier near $\phi=120^\circ$ from an O / side-chain methyl clash 4-5 bonds apart).

Katritch et al. described a "projection" approach to build an ICFF approximating a source Cartesian force field, using six-fold Fourier-series torsion corrections (to correct 1-4 repulsion) and a soft polynomial repulsion (to relieve 1-5 and 1-6 clashes), with fixed geometry from local Cartesian minimization. This reproduces the potential near minima well for evaluating conformation energies of small molecules, but to study large conformational changes (protein folding) via MD one must also recover the correct barriers (maxima), which depend on more than two torsion angles. The present work extends the projection idea to construct an ICFF with implicit flexibility suitable for TAMD of large protein conformational changes.

## Methods

### Recursive Solution of Equations of Motion

The recursive NEIMO algorithm of Jain et al. is implemented in a CHARMM TAMD module. Forces from the Cartesian force field propagate the system directly in internal coordinates; explicit gradients of the potential with respect to internal variables are not needed. The molecule is a branched tree of rigid bodies (clusters) with invariable internal atomic positions, connected by hinges carrying the internal coordinates. In this implementation hinges allow a single torsional DOF. One cluster is the base; adjoining clusters are parent (toward base) / child; tip clusters have no children. Each cluster has one origin atom (where the hinge to its parent originates) and non-tip clusters have branching atoms (where hinges to children originate). Efficient expressions are derived using spatial operator algebra; closed loops (e.g., disulfides) can be handled by extension.

For a serial chain of clusters (base = cluster $n{+}1$, plus $n$ torsion angles), the velocity, acceleration, and hinge force satisfy the Newton-Euler recursions eq:2-eq:5. The spatial velocity $V_k=\mathrm{Col}[\omega_k,v_k]$ is a 6-vector; $\phi_{x,y}$ is a $6\times6$ frame transform; $H_k^{T}=\mathrm{Col}[\hat h_k,0,0,0]$ is the hinge map with $\hat h_k$ the hinge unit vector; $F_k^{(c)}$ is the effective Cartesian spatial force (torque about cluster origin + net force from the potential, combining all atoms of the cluster); $F_k$ is the hinge spatial force; $M_k$ is spatial inertia about the origin; $a_k$, $b_k$ are gyroscopic/Coriolis terms; $T_k$ is the projection of the hinge force onto allowed DOF, which vanishes when all force-field forces are included in $F_k^{(c)}$.

Spatial operators express the recursions concisely (eq:6). Substituting the operator forms of eq:2-eq:4 into eq:5 yields eq:1 with the mass matrix given by the Newton-Euler operator factorization eq:7 and the velocity/force term eq:8. Solving for the acceleration requires inverting the $n\times n$ mass matrix; an efficient recursive inverse follows from the innovations operator factorization eq:9, giving the explicit inverse eq:10. Matrix $D$ is positive-definite and diagonal when all hinges have a single DOF, giving $O(n)$ inversion cost; the mass matrix need never be formed explicitly. Extension to a branched tree is straightforward: during tip-to-base recursion sum each cluster's child contributions before proceeding; during base-to-tip recursion continue separately along each branch.

The base cluster connects to the inert frame (or another tree) by a virtual 6-DOF hinge (hinge matrix = $6\times6$ identity), needing special treatment. Its $6\times1$ generalized velocity uses differentials of quasi-coordinates (integrals of angular velocity, whose only physical meaning is their time derivative). Base orientation is described by Euler angles or a quaternion; the quaternion equations of motion are solved with Fincham's implicit quaternion algorithm.

### Implementation in CHARMM

The CHARMM TAMD module has three facilities: tree-topology setup (automatic for standard residues, semi-automatic for nonstandard), energy minimization, and molecular dynamics. Internal coordinates are defined consistently with the tree. MD uses a modified leapfrog Verlet formulation (a simplification of the more rigorous implicit leapfrog integrator); energy conservation shows this is sufficient. Velocities are initially assigned from a Cartesian-space Gaussian distribution and mapped onto internal coordinates. Temperature control: a simple Berendsen thermostat plus velocity reassignment (Nose-Hoover chains could be implemented but are not expected to significantly change dynamics, particularly with REX). TAMD adds minimal computational cost; energy/force evaluation remains rate-limiting, and no bond/angle internal energy terms need evaluation (slightly reducing cost). Energy minimization does not involve the mass matrix; internal-coordinate gradients are obtained efficiently from Cartesian forces, then standard minimizers (steepest descent, Powell) apply.

### Towards an ICFF for Proteins with Implicit Flexibility

The projection idea of Katritch and coworkers is extended to construct an ICFF that accurately agrees with the original Cartesian force field and is suitable for MD of large protein conformational changes.

**Local fragments and dihedral crossterm corrections.** Both minima and maxima of the potential surface must be reproduced. Clashes between atoms separated by four or more bonds cause dramatic barrier increases depending on more than one torsion angle. To correct these: (1) use softened van der Waals and electrostatic interactions (unchanged until energies exceed thresholds, then linear soft-core form) - this introduces minimal disturbance to normal protein interactions while reducing barrier increases to a manageable range; (2) correct the residual barrier increase with dihedral crossterms via the CHARMM CMAP facility, computed from local torsion-crossterm fragments.

The backbone $\phi/\psi$ crossterm fragment is `-CO-NH-C_\alpha H-NH-CO-`. Two-dimensional grid-based correction maps are the difference between fully relaxed (extensive restrained Cartesian minimization) and fixed-covalent-geometry potential surfaces. A single $\phi/\psi$ map is shared by all residues except proline; use of these crossterms requires fixed peptide planes. Backbone/side-chain clashes are corrected by additional $\phi/\chi_1$ and $\psi/\chi_1$ maps; standard residues are grouped by $C_\beta$ connectivity (e.g., Val and Ile share maps). The side-chain fragments are `-CO-NH-C_\alpha-C_\beta H(X-)(Y-)` and `C_\beta H(X-)(Y-)-C_\alpha-NH-CO-`. Atomic charges are neutralized when bonded atoms are deleted from a fragment (e.g., Val $C_\gamma$ charge set to 0.0 from -0.27e; Ser/Thr $O_\gamma$ to -0.23e from -0.66e after deleting H of charge 0.43e). Corrections are applied at the residue level with no per-sequence tuning. The three maps together accurately reproduce the backbone $\phi/\psi$ and side-chain $\chi_1$ torsional surfaces. Barrier increases due to 1-4 vdW repulsion affecting side chains other than $\chi_1$ are minor and were not corrected.

**Fixed covalent geometry.** The optimal fixed geometry is application-dependent. A geometry from local Cartesian minimization is not representative of structures undergoing large conformational changes; specific backbone covalent geometries are required to form compact secondary structure (e.g., $\alpha$-helix). The fixed backbone geometry is therefore optimized to allow $\alpha$-helix formation; preliminary studies show minimal impact on $\beta$-strand formation and native $\beta$-structure stability.

### Simulation Protocols

All simulations used CHARMM with the PARAM22 all-atom force field. Dipeptide models: 100-ns NVT simulations to examine backbone/side-chain equilibrium and dynamics (Nose-Hoover thermostat in CMD, Berendsen in TAMD). NVE simulations studied energy conservation for systems of varying size/topology: Met-enkephalin (Ace-Tyr-Gly-Gly-Phe-Met-NMe, collapsed coil); (Val)$_{10}$ ($\alpha$-helix); GB1 (56-residue B1 domain of protein G, native $\alpha/\beta$); DHFR (159-residue, native $\alpha/\beta$ with long loops). Native GB1/DHFR unstable in vacuum -> weak backbone harmonic restraints (0.1 kcal/mol/Å$^2$). No nonbonded cutoff unless specified. SHAKE fixed H-bond lengths in all CMD.

Peptide folding and NMR refinement REX MD used the MMTSB Tool Set. GBSW implicit solvent for refinement; a related GBSW implicit membrane for WALP folding. In replica exchange (REX), multiple copies run at different temperatures (usually exponentially distributed), exchanging temperatures via a Metropolis-type criterion after some steps, reducing local-minimum trapping.

## Results and Discussion

### Quality of $\phi/\psi$ and $\chi_1$ Potential Surfaces in ICFF

Using dipeptide models, the extended projection approach accurately reconstructs the whole potential surface (minima and maxima). For valine dipeptide: softened vdW/electrostatics first significantly reduces barriers; remaining distortions are removed by crossterm corrections ($\phi/\psi$ handles barriers near $\phi\sim0^\circ$; $\phi/\chi_1$ and $\psi/\chi_1$ handle regions near $\phi=120^\circ$). RMSD between final ICFF and original map: 0.67 kcal/mol (low-energy region, within 5 kcal/mol of global minimum) and 1.6 kcal/mol (whole surface). Similar for all standard residues except proline. The $\chi_1$ torsion profile explicitly depends on backbone $\phi,\psi$ (making straightforward corrections inapplicable), yet ICFF $\chi_1$ profiles are essentially identical to originals for all backbone conformations.

### Conformational Equilibrium and Dynamics of Dipeptide Models

100-ns room-temperature MD in both torsion and Cartesian space. Hydrogen mass increased to 6.0 in all TAMD. Conformational equilibrium and flexibility are well restored in the ICFF for all dipeptides except proline (which loses $\phi$ and $\chi_1$ flexibility entirely). Dipeptides in the ICFF are at least as flexible as in the original Cartesian force field; more transitions between energy-minima states were observed in TAMD (especially at 300 K), suggesting enhanced sampling from exclusive sampling of torsional DOF. More rotamer transitions occurred in TAMD despite slightly elevated barriers from uncorrected 1-4 vdW repulsion.

### Energy Conservation Properties

Integrator accuracy is measured by total-energy fluctuation in NVE (eq:11). TAMD conserves total energy much better than CMD - fluctuations smaller by two to three orders of magnitude in all cases. With SHAKE on H-bonds, CMD conserves energy well up to 3 fs and diverges quickly above 5 fs. TAMD conserves energy well up to 10 fs (small peptides) and 5 fs (larger proteins), and does not diverge even at 20 fs. CMD/TAMD fluctuation depend differently on system size and compactness. Increasing all hydrogen masses significantly improves TAMD accuracy for small-to-medium systems (enabling 10 fs even for compact GB1) but is less effective for large compact systems like DHFR. Long-term energy drift (linear fit over 100 ps NVE) in TAMD is small: below 0.1 kcal/ps for GB1 even at 5 fs, and lower than CMD at >=2 fs.

The differing time-step dependence reflects a hierarchy of fast motions: constraining H-bonds removes the fastest motions (up to 3 fs); constraining all bond/angle DOF extends to ~5 fs; beyond that, rotations of low-inertia groups (hydroxyl, methyl) and nonbonded heavy-atom collisions limit the step. In CMD with SHAKE, the accuracy limit is bond/angle DOF, independent of size/topology until ~5 fs. In TAMD, all bond/angle DOF are constrained, so accuracy limits are system-specific for all time steps. Increasing H mass suppresses tip-group rotations, leaving nonbonded heavy-atom collisions as the limit - hence smaller steps for large systems (DHFR) than medium ones (GB1) even at comparable compactness.

### Folding of Simple Peptides

For simple peptides like poly-alanine (Ala)$_{10}$, time steps up to 20 fs can be reliably used, giving at least 10-fold enhancement over typical CMD. Internal-coordinate DOF are ~1/10 of Cartesian, so REX needs fewer replicas for the same temperature range (optimal replica number $\propto\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{dof}}}$), further reducing cost. Increase in time step is more limited for peptides with bulky side chains: (Val)$_{10}$ shows faster initial RMSD decrease under TAMD but spends more time in "nearly folded" intermediates (backbone RMSD ~2.0 Å), a trap that worsens with larger time steps. WALP16 (Ace-GWW(LA)$_5$WWA-NMe) in GBSW implicit membrane shows more severe limitations: TAMD REX at 5 fs has trouble sampling fully folded helices. Re-examining energy conservation over representative folding conformations showed accuracy at 300 K does not deteriorate appreciably from 1 to 5 fs (only significant decrease at 10 fs for the most compact state), and remains reasonable up to 5 fs at 800 K. Thus efficient conformational sampling may limit the time step more than energy conservation alone suggests.

### Refinement of NMR Structures of a Protein Domain

Refinement of NMR structures of a heat-shock protein (HSP) redox-switch domain (two helices at right angles, a two-stranded $\beta$-hairpin, a C-terminal helix), using a sparse early-stage NOE set that conventional calculations could not resolve into a unique topology. REX/GB refinement (16 replicas, 300-600 K, 1.0 ps MD per REX step, 1000 REX steps, last 200 production; structured region residues 7-60). Larger time steps achieve similar structure convergence (see Table 1 in checks.md). TAMD ensembles show slightly larger backbone RMSD (>3.0 Å) than CMD (2.0-3.0 Å), differing mainly in compactness rather than tertiary fold. Time steps >=5 fs show limitations in sampling native states and ranking conformers, consistent with the folding simulations - energy conservation is not the limiting factor; rather, overall flexibility under the rigid-body approximation is still not as good as in Cartesian space, and highly collective torsion-space motions plus long-range collisions limit small local adjustments in compact states.

## Conclusion

An efficient recursive NEIMO algorithm for MD in torsion space is implemented in a CHARMM TAMD module. Torsion-space dynamics eliminates high-frequency bond/angle DOF, allowing larger time steps, and explicitly samples only the most relevant DOF. With Cartesian-based force fields the rigid covalent-geometry approximation introduces significant energy-surface distortions; an extended "projection" approach reconstructs local surfaces and restores implicit local flexibility using softened vdW/electrostatics plus torsion crossterm corrections (CMAP) from local fragments. The resulting ICFF accurately projects the original Cartesian force field for backbone $\phi/\psi$ and side-chain $\chi_1$. TAMD enables much larger time steps (up to 20 fs for simple peptides, more limited for compact/large systems) plus ~10-fold fewer DOF, giving several-fold sampling gains. Time steps may be more limited than energy conservation alone suggests, due to high motion collectivity and long-range collisions in torsion space. Choice of fixed covalent geometry needs further study. TAMD is most powerful combined with efficient implicit solvent and advanced sampling (REX), e.g., for ab initio protein structure prediction and NMR refinement.
